The brief

Digital infrastructure has a heat & waste problem.

Data centers are the backbone of the modern economy — and they're incredibly energy-intensive. They reject enormous amounts of low-grade heat, and they retire servers long before those machines are used up. Both are wasted resources with a real carbon cost.

Over one weekend, teams build tools that quantify that impact and prove the upside of doing better.

Common thread · both tracksEvery project simulates the carbon footprint and environmental impact of a data center — then makes the case for reuse in numbers a city planner would believe.

Two tracks. One mission: provable sustainability.

Both briefs are still being finalized with our partners — here's where they're headed. Full packets drop at kickoff.

Track 01

Waste Heat Reuse

Draft · brief evolving

Build an interactive simulator that lets engineers model integrating waste heat reuse into a data center — capturing rejected heat for district heating, greenhouses, or aquaculture instead of dumping it.

  • Input baseline specs: IT load, PUE, WUE, cooling tech, climate
  • Model heat-pump uplift vs. water & energy saved by skipping cooling towers
  • Output adjusted PUE, Energy Reuse Factor, and CO₂e avoided
  • Judged on accuracy, UX, innovation & completeness
Data & Formula Packet provided
Track 02

Second-Life Hardware

Draft · brief evolving

Servers get retired before their useful life runs out. Build a tool that extends the life of OCP hardware through reuse and refurbishment — and wrap it in a business model that keeps gear in circulation.

  • Estimate a server's footprint from components + manufacturing
  • Assess how much useful life remains in the hardware
  • Recommend reuse opportunities that cut waste and cost
  • Grounded in AI, public datasets & OCP specs
Data & Formula Packet provided

Questions
Do I need a background in data centers or thermodynamics? +
No. We provide a Data & Formula Packet with the benchmarks, emission factors, and validated formulas. If you can code or work with data, you can build a strong project.
Who can participate? +
The hackathon is open to all undergraduate students in NYC, competing in teams of four. Don't have a full team? Sign up on the waitlist and we'll help you find teammates.
Which track should I pick? +
Track 01 (Waste Heat Reuse) leans thermodynamics + simulation UX. Track 02 (Second-Life Hardware) leans lifecycle data + business modeling. Both center on simulating a data center's environmental impact — choose the story you want to tell.
What do I build with? +
Anything — a web app, a dashboard, even an advanced spreadsheet tool. You'll get pointed to public datasets like Electricity Maps, Boavizta, and the EPA USEEIO database.
